A century of human stature, 1900–1996

The Tape Measure Never Lies

Every country writes its history into the bodies of its children. Height is the most honest progress report a nation can produce.

Two boys are born the same year with near-identical genes. One grows up 20 cm taller than the other. Nothing in their DNA explains it — everything about their passports does. Healthcare, protein, sanitation, and equality leave a mark you can measure with a ruler. This is what a century of measurement reveals: height IS development.

The Mirrors — Same Genes, Different Rules

Comparing nations is always contaminated: different ancestry, different climate, different everything. So use the experiments history already ran. Take one gene pool, cut it in half with a border or a policy regime, and wait two generations. Whatever separates the halves cannot be genetic.

Eleven sliders cannot predict a human being, and this one has never met a doctor, a geneticist, or your children. In closing

Height Is a Receipt.Somebody Paid It.

Every centimetre in this deck was bought — with doctors, with protein, with sewers, with the decision that no child in the country would go without. And every centimetre missing was bought too, by somebody choosing not to.

01 · The finding
Between nations, genes explain almost nothing
Within a country, height is roughly 80% heritable. Between countries it is almost entirely environment. Korea proved it with a border. Our model confirms it with data: 11 development indicators, zero genetic information, R² = 0.96.
02 · The driver
GDP ranks only #7 out of 11
This is not a slogan, it is a model result. In the trained XGBoost ensemble physician_density is the #1 driver at 37% importance, followed by protein supply (13%) and breastfeeding (10%). GDP per capita comes 7th. The richest nation on the list — the United States — is the only one that stopped growing. Being wealthy doesn't make you tall. Investing in healthcare and food does.
03 · The consequence
Height is a development metric
Height is not vanity, it is a biological summary of a generation's policy decisions — a single number that encodes healthcare access, nutrition quality, inequality, and environmental safety. Measure the children. You'll know how the country is doing.
Your genes set the ceiling.
Your country decides if you reach it.
Which is the uncomfortable part. Nobody chooses their gene pool, and nobody chooses the country that feeds them as a child. The tape measure does not care about either — it only records what was provided. The Netherlands gained 18 cm in a century by doing ordinary things relentlessly: doctors everywhere, protein on every plate, a toilet in every house, and a floor under every family. None of it was clever. All of it was chosen.

Nothing in this deck argues that taller is better. It argues that height is the most honest progress report a nation can produce — a single number that encodes how a society treated its children — and that it is still being written, right now, in cohorts that have not finished growing.
Dataset: 196 countries × 27 birth cohorts (1970–1996) × both sexes = 12,152 observations, 11 environment features. Source: NCD-RisC Lancet 2020 (height at age 19), World Bank, Our World in Data / FAO.
Model: XGBoost, 5-fold bagged disjoint-fold ensemble, separate male and female models (no sex variable in features) — Male R² 0.960 · RMSE 0.99 cm | Female R² 0.958 · RMSE 0.88 cm | Average R² 0.959. Hyperparameters: max_depth 6 · learning_rate 0.05 · n_estimators 300 · subsample 0.8 · colsample_bytree 0.8 · min_child_weight 3 · gamma 0.1 · reg_alpha 0.1 · reg_lambda 1.0 · MIN_BIRTH_YEAR 1970 training cut-off.
Top features by importance (avg male+female): physician_density 37% · protein_g_per_day 13% · breastfeeding_exclusive_pct 10% · healthcare_pct_gdp 7.5% · gini_index 6.5% · pm25_ug_m3 5% · gdp_per_capita_ppp 4.5% · caloric_intake_kcal 4.4% · sanitation_pct 3.8% · urbanization_pct 3.5% · animal_protein_pct 4%
Key design choice: Country identity is deliberately excluded from features — the model must explain height using only the 11 development indicators without knowing which country a row belongs to. This proves that environment alone is sufficient.
Height data: (1) the model panel — 15 spotlight countries × 11 birth cohorts 1900–1996, adult stature, used by the door frame, the files and the mirrors; (2) the world map — NCD-RisC / Lancet 2020, mean height at age 19 for 196 countries shaded from the full dataset, 1985 and 2019. Tooltips on the map always quote the map's figure. Some smaller island nations cannot be shaded (absent from the Natural Earth 110m outline set).
All features are sourced from public data sourced: height — NCD-RisC (Lancet 2020 child & adolescent country release, mean height at age 19) · caloric supply, protein supply and animal-protein breakdown — FAOSTAT via Our World in Data · GDP per capita PPP, Gini, physician density, healthcare % GDP, sanitation, breastfeeding, urbanization, PM2.5 — World Bank
No modelled or synthetic features. All 11 development indicators are real measurements from public datasets. No genetic proxies, no simulated data. The model uses only what was actually measured.
Research: Silventoinen 2003 · Schwekendiek 2009 (North Korean anthropometry) · Grasgruber 2016 · NCD-RisC 2016 · Jelenkovic et al. 2016.
Limitations: All associations are ecological — country-level relationships are not individual causation. Features with high missingness (sanitation 29%, breastfeeding 27%) are median-imputed; the signal survives but imputation adds noise. The childhood-window averaging (birth year → birth year + 10) uses closest available year as fallback when data is sparse.
